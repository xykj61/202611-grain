Dear Professor Jiang,

Warm greetings from the United States. I recently became a subscriber to your Substack after working my way through nearly all of your YouTube lecture playlists, and I wanted to thank you for the clarity and historical sweep you bring to your teaching.

In your recent lecture on why institutions appear to be unraveling, you offered three compelling explanations for the present turbulence. Writing as an American operating-systems designer with over twelve years of experience in systems architecture, I would like to offer a **fourth structural hypothesis** for your consideration -- one that looks beneath the surface chaos toward a deliberate, if high-risk, industrial and political realignment.

### 1. A Fourth Hypothesis: Controlled Stress-Testing Before 2028

Alongside the three dynamics you described, a fourth possibility is that key strategists within the governing Republican coalition are tolerating -- or actively accelerating -- a sharp near-term market and institutional stress test for three interlocking reasons:

1. **Consolidating AI and Capital Infrastructure:** Allowing an early shakeout consolidates frontier compute, semiconductor supply, and transnational capital into a smaller circle of domestic champions, much along the lines you have analyzed previously.
2. **"White-Hat" Stress-Testing Inherited Fragilities Now:** Taking the shock early -- exposing brittle legacy software, over-leveraged balance sheets, and neglected physical infrastructure at the start of the cycle -- creates a baseline from which visible recovery and rebuilding ahead of 2028 can be presented as proof of governing competence.
3. **Rebalancing Human Talent Toward the Real Economy:** Cooling speculative hype around white-collar software and AI nudges ambitious young graduates and working families back toward load-bearing vocations -- regenerative agriculture, civil engineering, domestic manufacturing, shipbuilding, and skilled trades -- where long-term family and national stability actually rest.

### 2. Why the Real Computing Revolution Has Barely Begun

Paradoxically, I write this as someone who is deeply optimistic about AI and computing. Yes, vast electricity and attention are currently burned on ephemeral memes, synthetic noise, and unverified "vibe-coded" software. Yet blaming the tool for its earliest, most frivolous uses misses a quieter revolution happening at the foundations of computer science.

Projects such as **TigerBeetle** -- a mission-critical financial accounting database written in **Zig** under strict deterministic safety bounds (*Tiger Style*: https://github.com/tigerbeetle/tigerbeetle/blob/main/docs/TIGER_STYLE.md) -- alongside high-throughput, energy-efficient verifiable ledgers (such as Solana, Sui, Aptos, Hyperliquid, and Monad) point toward computing's real maturity. When AI agents are guided by rigorous mathematical invariants and an explicit **Civic Style** (https://github.com/grain-ww/20260618-grain/blob/main/context/CIVIC_STYLE.md -- designing public and enterprise systems so that what is rewarded points directly at measurable human and ecological good), they stop producing brittle slop and become extraordinary instruments for **real-economy provenance**: verifying supply chains, auditing public works, and raising the trustworthiness of everyday goods and services.

Seen in that light, a great deal of current media alarm around AI's commercial profitability may miss the deeper point. Strategic compute does not need to justify itself on quarterly consumer-ad balance sheets if it functions as sovereign national infrastructure -- buying time while the United States and its partners attempt to re-shore defense, maritime, drone, and industrial capacity and protect global shipping lanes for critical minerals and semiconductors.

### 3. Sovereign Currency, Real Resources, and the Ecological Blind Spot

Where I find current conservative orthodoxy most in need of renewal is its neglect of **living ecology** and its attachment to household-budget myths about sovereign finance. Too many policymakers still speak as though a currency-issuing nation-state were a private household that could "run out" of its own unit of account, or as though ecological stewardship were the enemy of prosperity.

As economist **L. Randall Wray** and the **Modern Monetary Theory (MMT)** tradition demonstrate clearly, a sovereign state's public deficit is simply the accounting mirror of net financial assets invested into the non-government sector. Interestingly, earlier in my career I worked for Curtis Yarvin at Tlon on the **Urbit** operating system (https://urbit.org); yet even many brilliant technologists in Silicon Valley fall into the "hard-money" inversion of thinking gold or Bitcoin gives sovereign institutions value, when historically and practically it is sovereign law, courts, infrastructure, and maritime security that make markets in gold, commodities, or digital networks possible in the first place.

As Wray emphasizes, cryptocurrencies are not sovereign money -- and recognizing that truth actually *clarifies* what modern distributed ledgers are genuinely good for: **transparent, low-energy supply-chain integrity and public accountability**, while sovereign fiscal capacity funds **real resource efficiency** -- living soil, clean water, durable infrastructure, and human skill.

### 4. The 2026-2028 Heartland Opening: Civic & Ecological Rebuilding

Because the U.S. Electoral College weights smaller-population interior states heavily per capita (as illustrated clearly here: https://youtu.be/7wC42HgLA4k), whichever coalition between 2026 and 2028 speaks honestly and practically to working families in the American heartland -- pairing **sovereign public investment in dignified work, vocational training, and infrastructure** with **measurable ecological wealth** -- will hold a decisive advantage in 2028.

Even where congressional gridlock or bureaucratic inertia slows federal action, forward-looking industrialists, engineers, and venture stewards (from leaders like Elon Musk and Jensen Huang to thoughtful younger investors like Sarah Guo, alongside major capital allocators) have an extraordinary opportunity to partner in **bipartisan domestic rebuilding** across America's least-developed states. We already have the physical and architectural blueprints ready to hand:
- **Human-scale traditional urbanism** in the lineage of **Lewis Mumford** and **Christopher Alexander**;
- **Biocyclic vegan regenerative agriculture** pioneered by farmers like **Helen Atthowe** and **Frances Gonzales**, rebuilding topsoil and farm profitability simultaneously; and
- **Sovereign, right-to-repair domestic computing** -- RAM-only, USB-C-networked personal and enterprise machines that dramatically cut conflict-mineral demand and foreign supply-chain exposure while protecting household privacy by hardware design.

### 5. Strategic Patience and Multipolar Diplomacy

On the wider geopolitical chessboard -- including debates over whether maritime security and financial clearing in the Eastern Mediterranean and Red Sea might gradually shift toward regional partners, or whether older historical patterns of Levant-Persian commercial coexistence could one day re-emerge to lower global conflict -- the risks of miscalculation across the United States, Canada, the Middle East, and Eurasia remain sobering. Every grand geopolitical handoff carries red-flag failure modes if trust breaks down.

That is why, whether today's cascade of financial, cyber, and geopolitical shocks proves to be a consciously engineered stress test or an emergent storm, the wisest posture for builders over the next two years may be an **internal "American Switzerland" ethic**: calm, non-partisan, ecologically grounded, professionally rigorous, and devoted to keeping real communities, farms, and critical infrastructure whole while calling our wealthiest institutions toward genuine civic stewardship.

Thank you again for your lectures and for provoking such serious reflection across continents. I would be honored to hear your thoughts whenever time permits.

With grace, kindness, and devotion to The Beautiful,
