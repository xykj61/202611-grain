Today we are going to talk about a new application called Pulp.

Pulp lives inside Vantage -- the same single-brain UI that runs

## The Three Things Pulp Gives You

### 1. The Living Board

Every project in Pulp is a board. A board holds cards. Each card
is a single unit of work -- a task, a deliverable, a conversation,
a decision.

Cards move through columns. The columns are yours to name. You
might call them `Seed`, `Growing`, `Ripe`, `Harvested`. You might
call them `Design`, `Build`, `Witness`, `Ship`. Pulp holds the
shape; you fill the meaning.

Here is what makes a Pulp board different from every other task
board you have used: every card is also a visual canvas.

A card holds text, yes. It holds checklists and due dates and
assigned people, yes. And it holds a full rich visual frame --
a photograph, a sketch from Brushstroke, a clip from Resonance,
a waveform, a hand-drawn diagram captured straight from the
Carry stylus. The visual is the card. The card is the visual.

When you scroll a board, you see the work the way you see a
magazine spread. Each card is an image with a living heartbeat
underneath.

This is the first thing Pulp gives you: a project board that
looks like a wall of living art.

### 2. The Social Feed

Every board has a feed. The feed is a chronological river of
everything that happens on that board -- every card move, every
comment, every visual update, every voice note captured through
Earpiece, every witness result that lands GREEN or RED.

The feed looks like a social timeline. Each entry carries the
person's Kumara identity, the timestamp from One-Clock, and
a full visual preview of what changed. You scroll the feed the
way you scroll a photo journal. The work tells its own story
in time.

Here is the design choice that matters: the feed is append-only.

Every entry is a Blaze fact. Content-addressed, immutable, and
signed by the person who authored it. You cannot quietly edit
a feed entry after it lands. You can respond, clarify, or
supersede -- and the original stands. The project's history is
honest in the same way a ledger is honest.

When a team member opens the feed on a Monday morning, they
see every pulse of the weekend's work laid out as a visual
story. They already know where things stand before the first
word of a meeting.

This is the second thing Pulp gives you: a social feed that
turns every project into a visual, chronological, honest
narrative.

### 3. The Civic Canvas

Pulp is a Comlink application. That means every board, every
card, and every feed entry travels through the Comlink
network -- the same 405-council, 15-galaxy, Pond-routed
network that carries every other Grain message.

A board can be private to one person. A board can be shared
with a team of three. A board can be published to a galaxy
of citizens. The visibility is a single setting on the board,
and it uses the same capability fence that Pond enforces
everywhere else in Grain.

When you publish a board, its feed becomes a public civic
journal. Anyone in the galaxy can follow the project's
progress. They see the visual cards, the chronological feed,
the GREEN witnesses, the milestones. They see work happening
in daylight.

And here is the part that ties everything together: published
boards participate in Dimeroll.

A citizen who values a project can offer a HearthGrant --
the same bounded, transparent, receipt-carrying grant that
Linengrow already defines. The grant flows through Dimeroll's
`sum(nets) == 0` conservation law. The creator receives MALA
units. The Seva commons receives its transparent basis-point
split. The citizen receives a portable receipt they can carry
away at any time.

Patronage happens inside the same application where the work
happens. The person who follows a project's visual feed is the
same person who can directly support it. There is no separate
fundraising platform, no external payment processor, no ad
network sitting between the maker and the patron.

This is the third thing Pulp gives you: a civic canvas where
projects live in public, where honest work attracts honest
support, and where every transaction is as clean as a
double-entry ledger.

## How Pulp Looks on Station and Carry

### Vantage Station (macOS Desktop)

On Station, Pulp opens as a full desktop workspace. The left
rail holds your boards. The center holds the board view --
columns of visual cards, draggable, zoomable, with a Brushstroke
canvas always one keystroke away. The right rail holds the feed
for the selected board, scrolling chronologically.

When you are inside a card, the card expands to fill the center.
The visual canvas sits at the top. Below it: the checklist, the
conversation thread, the witness results, the Blaze fact chain.
Everything a project manager needs and everything a visual
storyteller wants, in one surface.

Resonance integration is native. A musician working on a track
can pin a Resonance session to a Pulp card. The waveform
renders live inside the card. When the mix bounces, the bounce
lands as a feed entry with a playable preview. Collaborators
hear the work inside the project board, inside the feed,
inside the same window.

### Vantage Carry (GrapheneOS Tablet and Mobile)

On Carry, Pulp adapts to touch and stylus. Boards become
vertical scrolls of visual cards -- each card a full-width
image with a text overlay. Swipe to move a card between
columns. Long-press to open.

The stylus becomes a first-class tool. Open a card, draw
directly on the canvas, and the sketch lands as a Blaze fact
in the feed. Carry's RLCD display shows the drawing with zero
ghosting at 60 fps -- the same write-only, never-read-back
Glasswall surface that every Vantage surface uses.

Voice notes go through Earpiece. The hardware mic switch and
bystander lamp gate every capture. The voice note lands in the
card's conversation thread and in the board's feed, with a
waveform preview and a full transcript via Flare inference.

On mobile, Pulp becomes the daily pulse. A citizen checks
their project feed the way a person checks a photo stream.
Each entry is visual, timestamped, and honest. The whole
project fits in a morning scroll.

## What Pulp Inherits from the Waymark Era

The elder Grain tree -- `20260618-grain` -- carried a project
called waymark ladders. Waymarks were four-letter words drawn
deterministically from a word list via SHA3-512 hashing, used
to name project ladders so they traveled cleanly through every
path and URL.

Pulp inherits the spirit of waymarks: each board carries a
unique, human-readable, deterministically-derived name. But
Pulp replaces the ladder metaphor with a living board, the
rung metaphor with a visual card, and the four-letter hash
name with a plain descriptive name that speaks clearly aloud.

The elder Linengrow prototypes -- `HearthGrant`,
`AudienceSplit`, `PortableReceipt` -- were the first draft of
civic patronage inside a project tool. Pulp weaves them into
the board as first-class features rather than bolt-on
integrations. When a patron offers a grant to a board, the
grant appears as a feed entry. When the grant's MALA flows,
the flow appears as a feed entry. The whole civic economy is
visible inside the same project narrative.

## The Principles Behind Pulp

**Sameness Is the Macro.** Pulp reuses the same Blaze fact
chain that powers Dimeroll, the same Kumara identity that
powers every Comlink handshake, the same Earpiece voice
capture that powers Resonance, the same RLCD Glasswall
surface that powers every Vantage display. Pulp adds zero
new foundational abstractions. It composes the ones that
already stand.

**Custody First.** Every card, every feed entry, and every
visual asset belongs to its creator. Kumara keys sign every
fact. A citizen can export their entire board -- every card,
every image, every conversation, every receipt -- as a
portable bundle at any time. The platform holds custody in
trust; the citizen holds the keys.

**Zero Ad/Telemetry SDKs.** Pulp runs inside Vantage's Civic
Glasswall. The display is write-only. The mic is hardware-
gated. There is no analytics SDK, no tracking pixel, no ad
network. Revenue comes from honest Dimeroll patronage, from
citizens who value the work enough to support it directly.

**TAME Bounds.** Every board has an explicit card ceiling.
Every feed has a bounded scroll buffer. Every Blaze chain
has a finite depth. Pulp runs inside the same bounded memory
model that every TAME module respects. There are no unbounded
lists, no infinite scrolls that quietly consume memory, no
feeds that grow until they degrade the device.

**Radiant Voice.** Every default label, every empty state
message, every onboarding prompt in Pulp is written in
Teacher + Radiant style. A person opening Pulp for the first
time finds warm, clear, encouraging language that trusts
their intelligence and welcomes them as a peer.

## One More Thing

Pulp is an application, and Pulp is also a directory.

`terrarium/pulp/` holds the platform glass for Pulp on
Station and Carry, following the same thin-host pattern
as every other Vantage surface: the logic lives inside
`modules/comlink/vantage/`, the host holds zero duplicated
app logic, and the display pipe is a dumb-glass I/O channel
to Vantage's single brain.

The name "Pulp" speaks clearly aloud. It is short, warm,
physical, and honest. Paper begins as pulp. A project
begins as raw material. Pulp is the room where raw material
becomes a living, visual, civic story.

Pulp is Husk boards made visual. Pulp is Dimeroll patronage
made personal. Pulp is Comlink networking made into a
project feed. Pulp is every Grain module composed into one
warm, inviting, deeply useful room where honest work lives
in daylight.
